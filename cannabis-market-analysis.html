Two Legal Blows Already Landed

1. Ohio SB 56 (already law): Ohio has passed legislation banning intoxicating hemp products in the state — the brand's home market. Their core revenue is already at risk in the state where they operate.

2. Federal "Closing the Loop" Law (effective November 12, 2026): Passed in late 2025 as part of a government funding deal. Closes the 2018 Farm Bill loophole that made hemp THC beverages federally legal. The transition window is finite.

The One Remaining Hope: Federal Carve-Out (Not Yet Law)

⚠️ Not a plan — a hope

A proposed amendment would protect low-dose hemp products: 5mg THC edibles and 10mg THC drinks. This has NOT passed as of the analysis date. Strategy cannot be built around it passing.

Strategic Paths Forward

Three paths exist. One recommended sequence.

Path A
Regulated Cannabis Channel
Enter adult-use cannabis markets as a cannabis-licensed beverage brand. Requires state licenses or co-manufacturing partnerships. High upside, high complexity.
⏱ Timeline: 12–18 months minimum
✓ Path B — Recommended Now
Hemp Beverage Continuity
Maximize hemp beverage sales in states that still permit them during the remaining legal window (through November 2026). Builds revenue and distributor relationships before the deadline.
⏱ Start immediately — window closes Nov 2026
Path C
Reformulation + Functional Pivot
Reformulate to federally compliant cannabinoids (CBD, CBN, CBG). Preserves brand and infrastructure long-term but gives up THC differentiation.
⏱ Timeline: 6–12 months to develop & launch
✓ Recommended Sequence

Path B now (9 months of hemp revenue in surviving states) running in parallel with Path A pipeline (Ohio dispensary licensing or co-manufacturing partner). Path C as insurance if Path A encounters regulatory delays.

🚨 Critical Pricing Issue

At $19.99/can, this brand is priced 2.5×–4× higher than every major competitor. The advisor needs a bulletproof answer to: "Why $19.99 when Wynk is $5?"

Available justifications: (1) Award credibility — only Double Gold award-winning THC beverage in the Midwest; (2) Craft/quality premium — local spring water, hand-trimmed, indoor-grown; (3) Per-mg value if dosage is significantly higher than competitors; (4) Early-stage brand pricing — artisanal production economics.

Recommended pricing move: Introduce a 4-pack at $59–65 (reducing effective per-can cost to ~$15) while maintaining $19.99 single-can flagship. Easier distributor pitch, better consumer economics.
